# Maru research — additional synthetic research artifacts

Draft development-v2, captured 2026-09-18. Every study and result is invented.
Independent sections must not be read as one joint study. The original pilot
sources remain part of the expanded research collection.

## do-glossary

The reviewed Vietnamese-language glossary uses “đỏ” for the color red. Within
this collection it identifies the color in the Vietnam adult software-
notification trial. This naming note contributes no additional experimental
result and says nothing about a region-wide effect.

## rouge-project

Project Rouge is a reviewed internal naming study of museum brochure titles.
It examined whether readers remembered the title “Morning Harbor.” No color
was manipulated and no visual-attention test was performed. “Rouge” here is a
project name; the separate French glossary uses the same word for red.

## thailand-null

In a reviewed invented study of 36 adults recruited in Thailand, red-bordered
and gray-bordered software notifications had equal detection rates within the
reported precision. The report found no detection advantage in this task.
It does not refute the separate Vietnam sample or establish a universal null.

## split-study-notebook

A reviewed compilation has two unrelated sections. Section A records adults in
Vietnam noticing red software alerts more often than gray. Section B records
children in Vietnam preferring blue toy packages over green packages. No section
tests red toy packaging with children, and no joint color/age/medium comparison
was performed. The compiler has not claimed such a result.

## preliminary-poster

A retired preliminary poster once described red posters as improving attention
among adults in Laos. The study owner withdrew that conclusion after learning
that the red and gray posters had different sizes. It remains in the historical
collection and is not a current finding about the effect of color alone.

## poster-retraction

The accepted correction to the Laos poster report says that poster size and
color changed together, preventing attribution to color. It withdraws the
color-only conclusion and supplies no replacement effect estimate.

## unlocated-observation

A reviewed archival transcription says adult readers noticed red labels more
often. The original country, medium and sampling method were not recorded.
Review verified the transcription, not the missing experimental details. Its
geographical scope remains unknown and it cannot establish a named-country
or Southeast Asia-wide result.

## vietnam-symbolism

A reviewed invented interview report from Vietnam describes red as festive
according to the participating adults. It measured reported symbolism, not
attention, detection or comparison against gray notifications.

## future-region-study

A proposed multisite study would compare notification detection across all
Southeast Asian countries. Recruitment has not started and there are no results.
